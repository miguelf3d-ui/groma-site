No. 14  —  Field notes  —  February 2026

Three days in the mills at Guimarães

The Ave valley has been spinning cotton since the 1840s, and for a stretch of the last century it clothed most of Europe. What remains is smaller and considerably better: a dozen family mills within twenty minutes of one another, nearly all of them run by the third or fourth generation of the same family.

We went in February to watch the Vela cloth come off the loom. It takes eleven minutes to weave a metre of it, and another two days for the finisher to decide it is ready. Nobody there described any of this as craft. They described it as the correct amount of time.
